Coraline 1.15.36 — dropper, lantern performance, and Festival follow-up

Minecraft 1.20.1 / Forge; Java 17.

Size: 66,861,858 bytes (66.86 MB / 63.76 MiB), slightly smaller than 1.15.35.

Replace the previous Coraline JAR; do not install two versions together.

Nether Fortress
- Five normal-gravity dropper courses use scattered horizontal beams, vertical
  basalt posts, Nether brick fences, and increasingly dense obstacles. They no longer
  consist of stacked solid floors with small holes.
- Lava targets shrink through 11x11, 9x9, 7x7, 5x5, and 3x3. Missing the target
  fails; lava inside the target advances the level. Blackstone is not the finish.
- Swept player-body collision detects side impacts and batched movement packets.
  Hidden gate-window checks no longer fail an otherwise obstacle-free fall.
- Safe teleport handoff grace, normal air steering, fire protection, owner-only
  scoring, rewards, portals, cleanup, and five-level lifecycle are reused.
- Live-client autopilot completed five actual normal-gravity falls and all four
  between-level handoffs without the old success-then-failure bug.

Moonlit lantern
- Removed per-tick full-volume lighting snapshots and full-volume diff scans.
- Immutable emitter snapshots use cached coarse occlusion fields, including
  intermediate wall checks, with fractional-position light sampling.
- Empty chunk sections are not rebuilt. Block relighting is queued, deduplicated,
  capped at two section requests per tick, and triggered by meaningful movement.
- Held light still brightens the mine; no moving invisible world blocks returned.
- Same-scene moving-player client measurements, 300 samples after warmup:
  no lantern: average 112.93 FPS; lighting tick mean 0.0295 ms, max 0.0873 ms.
  held lantern: average 111.42 FPS; lighting tick mean 0.0676 ms, max 0.3208 ms.
  These are one local test, not guarantees for every computer or shader setup.

Music Festival
- Hard limit: 180 seconds of performance, after the existing three-second countdown.
- Four rotating three-minute solo note-block arrangements: Minecraft, Clark,
  Sweden, and Subwoofer Lullaby. Existing recordings/masters are not truncated.
- Only a pressed note produces a musical note. No automatic backing/harmony layer
  and no off-key sound for a note the player merely misses. Wrong presses still
  produce off-key feedback. Crowd/score penalties for misses remain.
- A single confident melodic voice replaces the previous polyphonic detection
  layer. Sustained notes and short melodic repeats support the rising difficulty.
- Falling-note travel time decreases from 30 to 12 ticks during the performance.
- Notes per successive 45-second section:
  Minecraft:        40 / 69 / 96 / 127
  Clark:            37 / 69 / 93 / 132
  Sweden:           22 / 65 / 79 / 127
  Subwoofer Lullaby: 54 / 51 / 83 / 130
- Minimum spacing is five ticks; no simultaneous impossible input bursts.
- Existing rebindable keys, ownership, station lock, physical crowd, donation
  pool, legitimate reward delivery, and activity cleanup are preserved.
- Actual client check: 33 input presses, 33 performed harp sounds, and no original
  background recording. Early/finale highway and stage screenshots inspected.
- Limitation: these are automatically transcribed, simplified three-minute
  arrangements, not note-perfect full-length reproductions of the recordings.
  Exact musical fidelity and difficulty preferences still need player listening.
